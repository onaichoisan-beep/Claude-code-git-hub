## What's working

1. **Emotional, high-stakes moments.** The family reaction to the NEET result (485K) and the result-reaction Short (74K) are far ahead of everything else. Real reactions to a result that changes someone's life are the channel's strongest hook.
2. **NEET-season search traffic.** The answer-key/score video (14K) and expected-score video (7.5K) got views because people were searching those topics at the time. Timely NEET content brings in viewers.
3. **A clear, credible niche.** "Govt. MBBS student at NMCH documenting the journey" is specific and believable. Content like the medical camp, viva day and hostel room fits the niche well.
4. **Steady Shorts output.** The channel posts consistently, so the Shorts feed holds a stable 1–3K-view floor.

## What's holding it back

1. **The audience came for NEET, but the content moved to MBBS life.** The viral audience was NEET aspirants. Newer uploads are mostly generic college vlogs, and views fell from 10K+ to 1–3K. A recent long-form video (books, 246 views) got very few views.
2. **Very little long-form content.** There is only one long video in the last 8 months, and two of the "long" videos are really under a minute. Long videos are where watch time, subscribers and future ad income come from.
3. **Off-niche uploads blur what the channel is about.** Anime movie vlogs, the "dream I left years ago" space Short, dance and party clips, and comedy skits don't tell a new viewer what the channel offers. The channel keywords ("funny videos", "comedy", "dare challenge") don't match the About text (MBBS journey) either.
4. **Titles and hashtags are inconsistent.** Some titles use title case, some lowercase, some have typos ("beacuse"), and some are vague ("just kidding", "a moment come true…"). Vague titles don't help search or suggested-video traffic.
5. **The "#ad" NEET Short** ("How to CRACK NEET 2027…") is fine to post, but too many paid posts aimed at aspirants can cost trust with a young audience.

## Recommendations

1. **Make content for the NEET audience you already have.** Each week, post one video on "what I'd do differently for NEET", "NEET → MBBS reality check", "how I studied for my score", "Bihar/NMCH counselling explained", or "a day at a govt. medical college for aspirants". This is the audience that already watches.
2. **Post one real long-form video (8–15 min) every 2 weeks.** Good formats include a week in MBBS, how I study anatomy, a first-year exam debrief, a hostel/mess/fees breakdown, and answering aspirants' questions. Then cut 2–3 Shorts from each one and link them back to it with the "related video" feature.
3. **Plan for the NEET calendar.** Each year, plan reaction, answer-key, cutoff and counselling content ahead of the NEET result window (June). That's when this channel is most likely to see another 100K+ spike.
4. **Clean up the channel's positioning.** Change the keywords to medical/NEET terms. Keep comedy and anime in a separate playlist or post them less often. Pin the 485K video or a channel trailer, and create playlists such as "NEET 2025 Journey", "MBBS 1st Year", and "Books & Study".
5. **Use one title style everywhere.** Each title should state a clear benefit or stakes plus one keyword, e.g. "MBBS 1st Year Anatomy: 4 Parts You MUST Know". Fix typos, and use 3–5 relevant hashtags instead of #trending/#viral.
6. **Keep up the series that are working.** "MBBS 1st Year Books" (1.6K), "Anatomy" (1.4K) and "Subjects" (3.1K) are useful and easy to search. Make them a numbered series and expand the best ones into long-form videos.
7. **Use community posts and polls.** Ask aspirants what they want to know. Turn the answers into video ideas and pin comments that point to the next video.

## Bottom line

The channel already proved it can go viral (485K) with honest, emotional NEET moments, and it has an authentic niche. Its next step is to serve the NEET-aspirant audience it already has, with regular long-form, searchable "aspirant → MBBS" content, and to cut back on off-topic uploads.
