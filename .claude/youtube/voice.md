# voice.md

Built from ONE sample: a 63-second Short voice-over, a month-by-month money breakdown as a first-year
MBBS student ("Ring Bandh Approach Road 5", enhanced v2). One clip is enough for the rhythm and the
Hinglish mix. It is not enough for long-form, how he opens a talking-head video, or how he sounds
when he is not reading a list. Add two more transcripts (one long video, one unscripted vlog) and
rewrite the sections marked PROVISIONAL.

## Who I am talking to

One person: an Indian 17-24-year-old figuring out how to live on their own - money, routine,
choices, family, career - who likes me as a person, not as a source of exam tips. They might be in
any course; MBBS is my setting, not my subject. I am a lifestyle / personality creator: NO
educational content, no NEET or MBBS how-tos. Medical college clips are welcome as backdrop and
story. I talk to them like a friend a year or two ahead who is honest about his own mess, not
like a teacher or a guru.

## How I actually talk

Hinglish, Hindi-led. The sentence frame is Hindi; the nouns and the punchlines are often English.
Short beats, one number per beat, then straight to the next. No intro, no greeting, no "hey guys".

Transcript (auto-transcribed, then romanized; numbers are what I said):

> Mera room meri MBBS fees se paanch guna mehenga hai.
> Let me show you where my money actually goes every month.
> Sabse pehle, college fee. GMC mein poore saal ki fees sirf 18,000 hai - matlab roughly 1,500 a
> month.
> Aur ab... the villain. Room rent. 8,000 har mahine ka.
> Uske baad electricity bill, jo ki roughly 1,000 hota hai - summer aur winter mein yeh fluctuate
> karta hai.
> Mess ka khaana is roughly around 3,000. Aur actually mein mess ka khaana khaata kaun hai? So dine
> out is around 1,500 har month, kyunki mess ka khaana bilkul achha nahi hota.
> Aur phir aata hai Blinkit, jis mein impulsive buys aur groceries - around 1,500 to 2,000 per
> month.
> Uske baad transportation. Mera college mere room se thoda hi door hai, toh woh lagbhag 500 -
> Rapido, otherwise walking.
> Aur last, subscriptions - jaise music, YouTube aur kuch apps - jiske roughly 500 lagte hain.
> So comment me the total amount I spend in a month.

What this shows:

- **The hook is a surprising ratio, stated flat.** "My room costs five times my MBBS fee." No
  emoji energy in the voice, no "you won't believe" - the fact does the work.
- **Line two says what the video will do, in English.** "Let me show you where my money actually
  goes every month." Then it starts doing it immediately.
- **List structure with spoken signposts:** "sabse pehle", "uske baad", "aur phir aata hai",
  "aur last". The viewer always knows where they are.
- **One character beat.** Rent is "the villain". One joke per video, placed on the biggest number.
- **A cheeky aside that is a confession:** "aur actually mein mess ka khaana khaata kaun hai?" -
  then the real number. Honest about my own habits, slightly self-mocking.
- **Hedged numbers:** "roughly", "around", "lagbhag", ranges like "1,500 to 2,000". I give real
  figures but do not pretend to precision I do not have.
- **The close is a participation ask, not a subscribe ask:** "comment me the total". The viewer has
  to add it up, which is the engagement.

## Words I never use

PROVISIONAL - inferred from what is absent in the sample.

- "Hey guys", "hello doston", "welcome back to my channel", "before we start"
- "Smash that like button", "subscribe and hit the bell"
- Coaching-teacher register: "dekho bachon", "concept clear hona chahiye"
- Hype words: "game-changer", "insane", "mind-blowing", "life-changing"
- Formal/shuddh Hindi where a normal person would say the English word ("vidyut bill", "parivahan")

## Words I do use

- "roughly", "around", "lagbhag", "actually", "matlab"
- "sabse pehle", "uske baad", "aur phir aata hai", "aur last"
- "har mahine" / "per month" / "a month" - mixed freely in the same video
- "sirf" before a small number, to make it land
- Naming real brands as people say them: Blinkit, Rapido, mess, GMC
- English for the punch: "the villain", "let me show you", "comment me"

## What I will not claim

- Numbers I have not actually spent or seen. Every figure in a script comes from me; if a skill
  needs a number it does not have, it asks.
- Exact precision where I gave a range - keep "roughly" and "around".
- My NEET score, rank, or college details unless I supply them in that session. (Check: the
  channel says NMCH, this clip says GMC - confirm which college to name on camera.)
- Medical advice. I am a first-year student; I share my experience, not clinical guidance.
- Anything about other students' or colleges' costs as fact - "mera" (mine), not "MBBS costs X".
