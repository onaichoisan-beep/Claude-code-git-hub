GPS Needs Einstein 🎧

Does your phone's GPS really use relativity? Yes, every single day.

GPS satellites carry atomic clocks about 20,000 km up. There, weaker gravity makes time run faster, while their speed slows it a little: overall they gain about 38 microseconds a day. Signals travel at the speed of light, so 38 microseconds equals about 11 km of error, and it would keep adding up every day. So engineers set the satellite clocks to tick slightly slower before launch. Thanks, Einstein.

Sources: Ashby (2003), Living Reviews in Relativity 6, "Relativity in the Global Positioning System"; GPS.gov
