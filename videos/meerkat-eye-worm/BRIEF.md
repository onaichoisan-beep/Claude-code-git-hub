# The Bird That Pulled a Worm From a Meerkat's Eye (36.9 s)

Remake of a viral clip (a yellow weaver-like bird pecks at a meerkat's eye and pulls out a huge pale grub),
in the house style, with an honest twist.

**Story:** hook (grub yanked out) → rewind glitch → meerkat on guard, "nothing seems wrong" → the bird swoops
onto its head and keeps pecking the same spot → meerkat: "Oi! That's my eye, mate!" → bird: "Something's moving
in there." → pull… pull… a parasite pops free (flash) → meerkat: "Was that inside me?!" → fact: eye worms are real,
flies spread them while sipping tears → ruler: real ones are thread-thin, under 2 cm; the clip's fat grub is
"probably AI" → bird to the viewer: "Want me to check yours?" → loop flash back to the hook.

**Voices:** narrator en-GB-ThomasNeural (documentary), meerkat en-AU-WilliamMultilingualNeural (cheeky, small,
pitched up), bird en-US-AnaNeural (tiny, high).

**Sound:** braam + OGA CC0 slime squelches on the pulls, impacts on the pops, rewind + glitch, foley chirps,
squawk, wood-hit pecks, synth fly buzz, bubble tear, wind + crickets savanna bed.

**Facts:** Thelazia eye worms live on the eye surface of mammals (incl. dogs, foxes, people), are spread by
fruit flies/face flies feeding on tear secretions, and adults are thin, roughly 0.5–2 cm long.
The source clip's thick segmented grub is far bigger than that; we say "probably AI", not "fake".
Sources: CDC DPDx "Thelaziasis"; Otranto & Traversa (2005) Parasitology Research review of Thelazia;
WHO/ECDC notes on Thelazia callipaeda in Europe.
