Topic: octopus camouflage, told as a hunt (vertical pixel-art science short, ~30s, no voiceover).
A crab walks across a reef past a rock that turns out to be an octopus.
Facts: octopus skin has thousands of chromatophores (~10,000 colour cells; pigment sacs stretched by muscles),
plus iridophores/leucophores reflecting light; papillae raise the skin into bumps and spikes to match texture;
the change takes a fraction of a second (as fast as ~0.2 s); octopuses are colourblind (one photoreceptor type)
yet match colours — skin opsins may help them sense light.
Beats: WHERE IS THE OCTOPUS? / crab enters / SKIN WITH ~10,000 COLOUR CELLS / IT CHANGES TEXTURE TOO /
IN UNDER 0.2 SECONDS / IT'S COLOURBLIND / loop to empty reef.
