In this town, every person is born with a clock on their wrist.
It shows exactly when they're going to die.
Most people get seventy years. Eighty, if they're lucky.
My name is Leo, and this morning, my clock said one day left.
I'm twelve. I'm not even sick. My mom saw it and started crying.
Leo, the clocks are never wrong.
I ran to school to tell my best friend, Mia. She grabbed my arm.
Leo, look at everyone else's clocks.
The baker had two days left. The mailman had three. Every kid in my class had less than a week.
Then I noticed something strange. Every time the old clock tower rang, everyone's clock jumped down.
Everyone except one man. The mayor. His clock said nine hundred years.
That night, Mia and I snuck into the clock tower.
Inside, thousands of glowing hourglasses floated in the air. One for every person in town. Each one had a name on it. Mine was almost empty.
A thin golden stream was pouring out of every hourglass, straight into a giant clock in the middle of the room.
Suddenly, the doors slammed shut. The mayor stepped out of the shadows.
You shouldn't be here, Leo. Your time is up.
My clock started beeping. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight.
Leo, the gears! Stop the gears!
I spotted a huge lever beside the giant clock. Three seconds. I jumped and pulled it with everything I had.
The gears screeched to a stop. Every hourglass flipped upside down. The golden time poured out of the tower, back into every house in town.
My clock jumped. One day. Ten years. Seventy-five years.
The mayor screamed. His clock spun backwards, faster and faster, until it cracked, and he crumbled into sand.
The next morning, the whole town was outside, laughing and comparing clocks. Everyone had their years back.
I thought it was over. Until I looked at Mia's wrist. Her clock was blank. No numbers. Nothing.
She just smiled at me and whispered,
Leo, I was never born in this town.
