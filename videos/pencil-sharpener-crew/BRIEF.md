# Who's inside your pencil sharpener?

Pixel-art remake of a reference short (a tiny crew working inside a crank sharpener), in the house style:
30.0s, 1080×1920, cold-open hook, rewind glitch, small centred captions, sound effects only, loop ending.

**Message:** a playful "crew" gag, with real pencil facts in the captions and a reality twist at the end.

## Story
1. 0–1.8 Hook: a pencil smashes into the workshop and the crew scatters. "WHO'S INSIDE YOUR PENCIL SHARPENER?"
2. 1.8–5.2 Desk: the pencil goes in, the crank turns, then a dive into the window.
3. 5.2–7.0 Gears: "THE CRANK TURNS A RING OF GEARS".
4. 7.0–9.4 A T-rex roars, the fox sprints on the treadmill: "FOX POWER: ON".
5. 9.4–16.4 Beaver chews ("WOOD IS USUALLY CEDAR"), lumberjack chops the cone, a worker files the tip ("NO LEAD, JUST GRAPHITE + CLAY").
6. 16.4–23.2 Inspector test: the thick line gets a red X, the crew goes faster, then a fine line: "PERFECT POINT". The POINT meter at top left tracks progress.
7. 23.2–26.0 Back on paper: the girl hatches a drawing, "GRAPHITE IS BRITTLE…", SNAP.
8. 26.0–30.0 "NOT AGAIN!" The crew panics as light floods the hole. Twist: "REALITY: GEARS SPIN SPIRAL BLADES". Loops into the hook.

## Facts
- Most pencils are made from incense-cedar (cheaper ones use basswood and similar woods).
- Pencil "lead" has never been lead. It is graphite mixed with clay, and the clay ratio sets the hardness.
- In a crank (planetary) sharpener, the crank drives gears that spin helical cutters around the pencil, shaving it into a cone.
