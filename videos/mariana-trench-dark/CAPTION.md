Where Sunlight Never Reaches 🌊🔦🎧

Is there a place on Earth that sunlight never reaches? Yes: the bottom of the Mariana Trench.

Challenger Deep, in the Pacific Ocean, is about 10,935 m (nearly 11 km) down: Mount Everest (8,849 m) would fit inside with about 2 km of water to spare. Sunlight fades to about 1% by 200 m and is gone completely by about 1,000 m. At the bottom the pressure is over 1,000 times what you feel at sea level, enough to squash a foam cup to a fraction of its size. And yet life is there: snailfish swim at around 8,000 m, and tiny amphipods and single-celled xenophyophores live on the floor of Challenger Deep, in total darkness.

Even in the darkest depths of our planet, life finds a way. Would you go down? 👇

Sources: NOAA Ocean Exploration & NOAA Ocean Service; Greenaway et al. (2021) Geoscience Letters; Gerringer et al. (2017) Zootaxa (Mariana snailfish)
