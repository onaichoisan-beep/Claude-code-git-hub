## Intent

Bright pixel-art true-story short in the user's style, built autonomously. Hook: bloodshot twitching eyes,
"This teen stayed awake for 11 days straight". An hours-awake counter (with day number and progress bar)
sits upper-left. Story: Randy Gardner, 17, science fair (Dec 1963-Jan 1964) → day 2 eyes stop focusing →
sleep washes the brain clean, without it waste piles up → day 4 hallucination (street sign becomes a person;
he believed he was a pro football player) → parts of the brain fall asleep while awake (local sleep) → day 11
he couldn't count down from 100 by sevens (stopped at 65) → slept ~14 hours and recovered → "Don't try this." → loop.

## Notes

- Record: 264.4 hours (11 days 24 min). Observed by sleep researcher William Dement. Guinness stopped
  accepting sleep-deprivation records because of the danger.
- Glymphatic clearance during sleep (Xie et al., Science 2013); local sleep in awake brains (Vyazovskiy 2011,
  Nir 2017). Hallucination of a street sign as a person and the football-player belief are from the
  standard accounts of Gardner's experiment.
