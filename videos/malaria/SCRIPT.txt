One mosquito bite is all it takes. As she drinks your blood, her saliva slips dozens of tiny parasites into your skin. In under an hour, they ride your bloodstream straight to your liver, sneak inside your liver cells, and hide. For about a week, you feel totally fine, while each parasite quietly copies itself into tens of thousands more. Then the liver cells burst, flooding your blood with new parasites, and each one invades a red blood cell. Inside, it eats the hemoglobin and multiplies again, until the cell explodes and releases even more, which attack new red blood cells. Every time millions of cells burst at once, parasite waste floods your blood, and your body fights back with waves of fever, chills, and sweating. And when the next mosquito bites you, it sucks up the parasites, and the whole cycle starts again.
