## Intent

The owner's own take on a viral fission video (same topic, retold in the house style, not copied):
cold-open on the fireball, "One invisible particle did this" → rewind → a neutron hits a Pu-239 nucleus →
it wobbles, stretches and splits into Xe-134 + Zr-103 + 3 neutrons → a branching chain-reaction tree with an
upper-left "atoms split" counter (2 → 2.4×10^24 after 800 ns) → whiteout → Trinity, July 16 1945, 5:29 AM,
New Mexico: fireball, mushroom cloud, ~25,000 t TNT → sand melted into green glass (trinitite) → twist:
the mass that vanished was ~1 gram, the weight of a paperclip (E=mc²) → loop: the next neutron flies in.

Differences from the reference: plutonium (what Trinity actually used) instead of uranium, a branching tree
instead of a dot field, centred captions instead of top labels, the trinitite beat and the paperclip/scale twist.

## Facts & sources

- Pu-239 + n → e.g. Xe-134 + Zr-103 + 3n (one possible split; Pu-239 averages ~2.9 neutrons per fission).
- Generation time ~10 ns ("a shake"); ~80 generations in under 1 µs (Atomic Archive / Los Alamos histories).
  The counter is idealised doubling: 2^81 ≈ 2.4×10^24, about the number of atoms in ~1 kg of Pu-239, roughly
  what fissioned in the Gadget's 6.2 kg core.
- Yield: 24.8 kt TNT (Selby et al., Nuclear Technology 2021). E = mc² → ~1.1 g of mass converted.
  A standard paperclip is ~1 g.
- Detonation 05:29:45, 16 July 1945, Jornada del Muerto, New Mexico. Trinitite: green glass from melted desert sand.
